ACT pool fencing and approvals in Canberra

In short: A pool in the ACT that can hold more than 300 mm of water generally needs building approval, a licensed builder and certifier, and a safety barrier that meets current standards.

A summary of official rules, with links to the source pages. It is not legal advice.

Last updated: 23 November 2026

This page summarises official ACT Government information so you know what to expect. It is not legal advice and doesn't replace a licensed certifier. Follow the links and ask the builder or certifier if you're unsure.

The short answer

ACT Planning says a swimming pool that can hold water deeper than 300 mm generally needs building approval, with a licensed builder, a licensed certifier, plans and inspections. Pool fences may not need separate building approval if they comply with the Building Code and are inspected and certified by a licensed certifier.

Source: ACT Planning, Check if you need a building approval.

Development approval

Some pools also need development approval, depending on the block and the design. Check the ACT Planning DA checker or ask your builder.

Local council: Canberra

Canberra has no local council; planning and building approvals are run by the ACT Government through ACT Planning and Access Canberra. Older suburbs and new Molonglo or Gungahlin estates can have different requirements, so give the builder your block details early.

The ACT pool safety reforms

New ACT rules for home swimming pools and spas started on 1 May 2024. They apply to pools and spas that can hold more than 30 cm of water and are linked to a home, including portable and above-ground pools. Since 1 May 2024, owners must keep barriers maintained and keep doors, gates and covers to the pool closed when it isn't in use. All home pools and spas must have a barrier that meets current standards by 1 May 2028. Pool information must also be disclosed when a property is sold or leased.

Source: ACT Planning, Home swimming pool safety reforms.

Licences

In the ACT, building work that needs building approval has to be done by a licensed builder, with a licensed building certifier overseeing the approval and inspections. Access Canberra keeps the public register of licensed builders and certifiers, so you can check a licence before you sign.

Source: ACT Planning, Check if you need a building approval.

Official pages checked 23 November 2026. Rules can change; check the linked sources or a licensed certifier before relying on this summary.
